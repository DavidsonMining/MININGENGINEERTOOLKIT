🧪 Mineral Processing

A practical overview of mineral processing, ore preparation, size reduction, concentration, separation, dewatering and recovery.

1. What is Mineral Processing?

Mineral processing is the series of physical and sometimes chemical processes used to separate valuable minerals from unwanted material after mining.

The main objective is to produce a concentrate or valuable product with suitable characteristics while rejecting as much unwanted material as practical.

Mineral processing connects mining with the production of a usable mineral product.

2. The Mineral Processing Flow

⛏️ Run-of-Mine Ore

Material delivered from the mine is commonly referred to as run-of-mine ore.

🪨 Crushing

Large pieces of ore are reduced in size to make the material suitable for subsequent processing.

⚙️ Grinding

Further size reduction may be carried out when the valuable minerals need to be liberated from the surrounding material.

📏 Classification

Material may be separated according to particle size using equipment such as screens and hydrocyclones.

🔬 Concentration

Separation processes are used to increase the proportion of valuable minerals in the product.

💧 Dewatering

Water is removed from concentrates or other process streams using equipment such as thickeners and filters.

📦 Final Product

The processed material may be transported, stored or sent to a further stage such as smelting or refining, depending on the mineral being produced.

3. Ore Preparation

Ore preparation involves preparing mined material for efficient separation.

Important preparation activities may include crushing, screening, washing, blending and grinding.

Proper preparation helps ensure that the feed entering the separation stages has suitable particle size and characteristics.

4. Crushing

🪨 Primary Crushing

Primary crushing reduces the size of large run-of-mine material.

⚙️ Secondary Crushing

Secondary crushing provides additional size reduction after primary crushing.

🔧 Tertiary Crushing

Tertiary crushing may be used when further reduction is required before grinding or another processing stage.

Common crushers include jaw crushers, gyratory crushers, cone crushers and impact crushers.

5. Grinding

Grinding reduces crushed ore to a finer particle size.

The main purpose is often to achieve sufficient liberation of valuable minerals from the surrounding gangue material.

Common grinding equipment includes ball mills, rod mills and other specialised grinding systems.

6. Mineral Liberation

Mineral liberation refers to the degree to which valuable mineral particles have been separated from unwanted minerals through size reduction.

Adequate liberation is important because separation processes generally perform better when valuable minerals are sufficiently liberated.

Excessive grinding, however, can produce unnecessary fines and increase energy consumption.

7. Screening and Classification

📏 Screening

Screening separates particles according to size using a screen or similar mechanical device.

🌊 Classification

Classification separates particles according to their behaviour in a fluid, commonly water.

Screens are commonly used for coarser material, while hydrocyclones and similar equipment are commonly used for finer particles.

8. Concentration

Concentration is the process of separating valuable minerals from unwanted minerals using differences in their physical or surface properties.

The choice of concentration method depends on the characteristics of the ore and the valuable mineral.

9. Gravity Separation

Gravity separation uses differences in particle density and movement under gravitational or enhanced gravitational forces.

It can be useful when valuable minerals have a significant density difference from the surrounding material.

Examples of gravity equipment include jigs, shaking tables, spirals and centrifugal concentrators.

10. Magnetic Separation

Magnetic separation uses differences in magnetic properties to separate minerals.

Magnetic separators can be used to recover magnetic minerals or remove unwanted magnetic material from a processing stream.

11. Flotation

Froth flotation is a separation process based on differences in the surface properties of minerals.

In a flotation circuit, selected mineral particles attach to air bubbles and are carried into a froth phase, while other particles remain mainly in the pulp.

Flotation is widely used for the concentration of many sulphide ores and other minerals.

12. Dense Medium Separation

Dense medium separation uses a fluid or suspension with a controlled density to separate particles based mainly on density differences.

Particles with different densities behave differently in the separation medium, allowing valuable and unwanted material to be separated.

13. Dewatering

💧 Thickening

Thickening increases the concentration of solids in a slurry by allowing particles to settle and water to be removed.

🧺 Filtration

Filtration removes additional water from a concentrated solid stream using a filter medium.

Dewatering can reduce the moisture content of products and make them easier to handle and transport.

14. Concentrate and Tailings

💎 Concentrate

Concentrate is the product stream containing an increased proportion of the valuable mineral after processing.

🪨 Tailings

Tailings are the remaining material rejected from the processing circuit after valuable minerals have been recovered to the required extent.

15. Recovery

Recovery describes the proportion of the valuable material in the feed that is recovered into the desired product.

High recovery generally means that a larger proportion of the valuable mineral has been recovered from the feed.

Use the Mining Engineer Toolkit's Recovery Calculator for basic recovery calculations.

16. Grade

Grade represents the concentration of a valuable component in a mineral product or ore.

Examples include percentage copper, percentage iron, grams of gold per tonne of ore, or other appropriate units depending on the commodity.

Grade and recovery are important measures when evaluating mineral processing performance.

17. Mass Balance

A mass balance tracks the amount of material entering and leaving a processing system.

In a simplified processing circuit:

Feed = Concentrate + Tailings

More detailed balances can also track valuable components, water, intermediate streams and multiple products.

18. Processing Plant Performance

Processing plant performance can be evaluated using several indicators.

  • Feed rate
  • Product grade
  • Recovery
  • Concentrate production
  • Tailings production
  • Particle size distribution
  • Water consumption
  • Energy consumption
  • Equipment availability

19. Factors Affecting Mineral Processing

  • Ore mineralogy
  • Mineral liberation
  • Particle size
  • Mineral density
  • Surface properties
  • Ore hardness
  • Clay content
  • Water quality
  • Feed grade
  • Equipment performance
  • Operating conditions

20. Energy in Mineral Processing

Size reduction, particularly crushing and grinding, can require significant amounts of energy.

Efficient circuit design and appropriate equipment selection are therefore important considerations in mineral processing operations.

21. Mineral Processing and the Mining Cycle

Mineral processing is part of a larger mining and mineral production system.

Exploration → Mining → Crushing → Grinding → Separation → Concentrate → Further Processing

The quality of mined material can affect processing performance, while processing requirements can also influence mining and ore-management decisions.

22. Key Principle

Mineral processing is about separating valuable minerals from unwanted material as efficiently as practical.

A successful processing operation balances fragmentation, liberation, separation efficiency, recovery, product grade, energy consumption, water use, operating cost and environmental management.
